Title: The impact of founder personalities on startup success | Scientific Reports

Content:

**Investment Perspectives (Schools of Thought)**
*   **Supply-side/Product Investors:** Prioritize novel and superior products/services and intellectual property (e.g., patents, trademarks).
*   **Demand-side/Market-based Investors:** Prioritize high market interest, "hot" technology sectors (e.g., quantum computing), or large-scale social/economic challenges (e.g., decarbonization).
*   **Talent Investors:** Prioritize the founding team over the initial product or industry. The logic is that a high-functioning team can pivot to new markets or products if the initial offering fails to achieve product-market fit.

**Factors Influencing Startup Success/Failure**
*   **Team Composition:** 23% of tech startup failures are attributed to "not having the right team" (ranked as the third leading cause of failure).
*   **Firm-Level Factors:** Industry, location, and current economic conditions.
*   **Internal Team Factors:** Previous experiences/failures, centrality in global networks of founders and investors, and team size.
*   **Founder Personality Traits:**
    *   Successful entrepreneurs often exhibit a preference for variety, novelty, and starting new things ("openness to adventure"), higher activity levels (exuberance), and lower levels of modesty (preference for being the center of attention).
    *   There is no single "Founder-type" personality; research identifies at least six different personality types.
*   **Team Diversity:** Larger teams with diverse personality types show an increased likelihood of success.

**Founder Personality Traits and Startup Success**

*   **Correlation Between Personality and Success:** Specific "Big Five" personality traits, particularly **adventurousness** and **openness**, are significantly more widespread among founders of successful startups.
*   **Key Trait: Adventurousness:** Defined as a preference for variety, novelty, and starting new things. This is identified as the most significant facet distinguishing entrepreneurs from employees, aligning with the founder's role in exploring non-scalable activities and testing new business models/products.
*   **Other Notable Traits:** "Activity Level" (Extraversion) and "Modesty" (Agreeableness) were also identified as considerable effects in distinguishing entrepreneurs.
*   **Team Composition and Complementarity:**
    *   Multi-founder teams are more likely to succeed than solo founders.
    *   Success is significantly higher in teams with **diverse and complementary** personality combinations.
    *   Example of a high-odds success combination: An adventurous "Leader," a conscientious "Accomplisher," and an extroverted "Developer."

**Defining and Measuring Startup Success (Investment Logic)**

*   **Extrinsic Success Metrics:** The research defines success via "major capital liquidation events," which serve as objective signals that a venture has matured. These include:
    *   Being acquired.
    *   Acquiring another company (indicating scale, maturity, and capability).
    *   Initial Public Offering (IPO).
*   **Critique of Other Metrics:**
    *   **External Investment:** Noted as an unreliable measure of actual business success because it reflects *investor expectations* of potential rather than realized success.
    *   **Revenue Growth/New Products:** Can be short-lived or fail to find long-term traction.

**Predictive Value of Personality**

*   **Entrepreneur Identification:** Machine learning models using personality vectors alone could predict whether an individual was an entrepreneur or an employee with 82.5% accuracy.
*   **Stability:** Personality traits are assumed to be stable in adulthood and not significantly influenced by external life events, suggesting that founder personality drives startup success rather than success altering personality.

- Research Methodology: The study used agglomerative hierarchical clustering and the Big Five personality facets to categorize startup founders (n = 32k).
- Founder Typology: The research identified six distinct clusters of startup founders based on personality features.
- Personality-Occupation Correlation: The study utilized an occupation-personality matrix to map these clusters to "occupation tribes," suggesting that specific personality attributes associated with certain professions are present in startup founders.
- Cluster Composition: Three "purebred" clusters (#0, #2, and #5) were identified, where over 60% of individuals in the cluster share the personality attributes of a single occupation tribe.

**Founder Personality Types (FOALED Model)**
Research identifies six distinct personality types of startup founders:
*   **Accomplishers:** Organized, outgoing, confident, accommodating. Often serve as CEO, CFO, or COO.
*   **Leaders:** Adventurous, persistent, assertive, calm under pressure.
*   **Fighters:** Spontaneous, impulsive, tough, skeptical. Often serve as CTO, CPO, or Chief Commercial Officer.
*   **Experts/Engineers:** High in imagination and intellect.
*   **Developers:** Closest to roles such as Application Developers, Business Systems Analysts, and Product Managers.
*   **Operators:** High in orderliness (conscientiousness) and humility (agreeableness). Closest to service and maintenance management roles.

**Core Traits of Entrepreneurs**
Regardless of type, successful entrepreneurs share a central core of correlated features:
*   High intellect
*   High adventurousness
*   High activity level

**The Ensemble Theory of Success (Team Composition)**
The combination (ensemble) of personalities in a founding team significantly impacts the probability of success due to complementarity:
*   **Team Size:** Firms with three or more founders are more than twice as likely to succeed as solo-founded startups.
*   **Personality Diversity:** Success is linked to a broader range of maximum traits across the team. Successful teams tend to score higher on Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, and Agreeableness.
*   **High-Probability Combinations:** Three specific trio-founder combinations were found to be more than twice as likely to succeed as other combinations:
    1.  One Leader and two Developers.
    2.  One Operator and two Developers.
    3.  One Expert/Engineer, one Leader, and one Developer.

**Distinction from Employees**
Successful entrepreneurs can be distinguished from successful employees with over 80% accuracy using personality trait data alone, specifically regarding distributions of adventurousness, modesty, and activity level.

**Research Limitations/Biases**
*   **Data Sources:** Data from Crunchbase and Twitter may bias results toward externally funded companies, technology sectors, multi-founder teams, male founders, and companies that have already achieved some level of success.
*   **Digital Identity:** Personality traits were inferred from social media, which may reflect a curated digital identity rather than actual personality.

- Potential variables affecting startup success and investor logic:
    - Gender diversity of founders.
    - Geographic location (startups outside the US) and its impact on investor success metrics.
    - Generational differences in personality and outlook among entrepreneurs.
- Premise: Personality traits serve as both a predictor and a driver of success in entrepreneurship.

**Founder-Level Factors and Startup Success**
* **Personality Impact:** Founder character traits significantly impact a startup's likelihood of success, acting as a critical variable alongside traditional firm-level factors.
* **Founder vs. Employee Profiles:** Startup founders possess significantly different personality profiles compared to general employees.
* **Team Composition:** Personality-diverse teams have the most considerable impact on the probability of a startup's success, indicating that diversity in personality is a key driver of team performance.

**Research Methodology for Predicting Success**
* **Data Sources:** Research utilized Crunchbase (business information on early-stage startups) and Twitter (to infer personality traits via natural language processing).
* **Personality Metrics:** The "Big Five" personality domains (Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Emotional Stability) and 30 corresponding subdomains/facets were used to analyze founders.
* **Success Benchmarking:** For specific sub-analyses, "successful entrepreneurs" were defined as those whose companies attracted more than US$100k in investment.
