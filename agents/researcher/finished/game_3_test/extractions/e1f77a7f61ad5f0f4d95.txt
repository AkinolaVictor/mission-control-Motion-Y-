**Don Valentine's Investment Logic (Sequoia Capital)**

**Core Investment Philosophy**
*   **Market Primacy:** Believed that markets, rather than founders, dictate a company's ultimate scale.
*   **Market Selection:** Focused on identifying large markets early and backing companies with the capability to dominate them.
*   **Technological Inflection Points:** Targeted startups emerging at "inflection points" where industry disruption creates openings for new entrants.
*   **"Confusion Phases":** Sought companies operating in periods where large incumbents had not yet determined how new technologies would reshape their industries.
*   **Sector Preference:** Favored quickly growing technology markets over slower consumer sectors (a preference reinforced by unsuccessful investments in retail and grocery).

**Founder Evaluation Criteria**
*   **Intellectual Humility:** Favored "humble founders" and entrepreneurs who "knew what they didn't know," prioritizing adaptability over charisma.
*   **Analytical Rigor:** Used a Socratic questioning method to probe founders' assumptions, strategic priorities, and financial discipline.
*   **Conciseness:** Demanded brevity in business plans (e.g., requiring a proposal to fit on the back of a business card).

**Operational Logic and Discipline**
*   **Risk Management:** Emphasized disciplined spending and rigorous evaluation to avoid speculative excesses during market volatility.
*   **Geographic Concentration:** Preferred investing in Northern California (Silicon Valley), arguing that the concentration of opportunity there outweighed options elsewhere.
*   **Skepticism of Hype:** Remained skeptical of hype-driven trends (e.g., early "Information Highway" rhetoric) that lacked real business applications.
