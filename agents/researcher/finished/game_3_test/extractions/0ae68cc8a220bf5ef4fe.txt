Title: Bridging ethical and governance gaps in digital health: insights from users and startups in Iran | BMC Health Services Research | Springer Nature Link

Content:

Economic and Sustainability Gap: The study identifies "insufficient financial incentives and policy support" as one of six interrelated governance gaps affecting the development of the digital health ecosystem in Iran.

Factors impacting the sustainability and legitimacy of digital health startups in emerging economies (LMICs):
*   Regulatory and Oversight Gap: Fragmented authorities and a lack of unified licensing.
*   Ethical and Trust Gap: Weak accountability and declining user confidence.
*   Data Governance Gap: Unclear data ownership and poor privacy protection.
*   Quality and Standardization Gap: Lack of accreditation systems and evaluation indicators.
*   Institutional Capacity Gap: Low readiness for digital transformation and limited expertise.

Operational barriers for e-health businesses:
*   Legal and organizational barriers, including non-standardized licensing processes and weak monitoring mechanisms, constrain compliance and sustainable operation.

- Economic and sustainability gaps: Economic constraints and unclear reimbursement mechanisms act as barriers to ethical and sustainable operations.
- Tension between ethics and viability: Startup entrepreneurs report a conflict between maintaining ethical compliance and ensuring financial viability.
- Disincentives for ethical investment: Investing in secure systems or ethical review processes is perceived as a hindrance to speed, with a reported lack of financial incentives for prioritizing ethical practices.

Economic and sustainability gap:
- Economic fragility impacts the long-term viability and ethical behavior of digital health startups.
- Factors placing pressure on startups include:
    - Uncertain pricing mechanisms.
    - Limited insurance reimbursement.
    - Weak investment incentives.
- These economic pressures can discourage startups from investing in ethical safeguards.
- Evidence suggests that for responsible digital health ecosystems to be sustained, governance frameworks must align economic incentives with both innovation and compliance.
