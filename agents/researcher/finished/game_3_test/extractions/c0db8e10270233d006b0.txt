Title: A study of Chinese enterprises’ business models to determine the impact of dynamic capabilities on innovation and performance | PLOS One

Content:

**Factors Influencing SME Performance and Business Model Innovation (BMI)**

**Business Model Innovation (BMI) Logic**
*   **Definition:** Irreversible changes to a company’s business model, involving significant changes in infrastructure.
*   **Strategic Value:** BMI allows companies to maintain competitive advantage by generating activities beyond product and process innovation. It enables SMEs to adapt to market changes and environmental shifts more rapidly, increasing resilience to economic downturns and industry disruptions.
*   **Risks:** BMI is associated with high risk, uncertainty, and ambiguity. It may require companies to "cannibalize" current revenue streams to pursue uncertain future revenues.
*   **SME vs. Large Firm Dynamics:** Large companies can experiment with new business models via spinoffs without threatening survival. SMEs are less likely to experiment due to limited resources and the potentially "catastrophic impact" of BMI failure.

**Key Drivers of Performance (Dynamic Capabilities)**
*   **Enterprise Risk Management (ERM):** 
    *   A systematic approach to identifying, assessing, and responding to opportunities and threats.
    *   Impact on BMI: Enhances risk identification, strategic alignment, and resource allocation. It can uncover market gaps and emerging trends.
    *   Limitations: In smaller firms, the costs of ERM may outweigh the benefits; poorly executed ERM can become a bureaucratic exercise.
*   **Organizational Agility (OA):** The ability to swiftly perceive opportunities and innovate products/services to meet customer needs and respond to external environmental changes.
*   **Entrepreneurial Orientation (EO):** A behavioral model characterized by innovation, risk-taking, and proactivity. EO enables firms to proactively utilize resources to improve long-term financial performance.

**Interdependencies**
*   **Mediation:** Efficiency-centered BMI acts as a mediator between ERM, OA, EO, and overall SME performance.
*   **Synergy:** The combination of ERM (risk alignment), OA (flexibility), and EO (proactivity) creates a resilient organizational environment for thriving in uncertain markets.

**Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) and Investor Logic**
* **Investor Assurance:** Robust ERM practices provide a structured approach to managing risks, which reassures investors and can lead to higher market valuations and better credit ratings.
* **Cost of Capital:** Effective ERM is linked to a reduction in the cost of capital.
* **Financial Performance:** There is a positive correlation between ERM maturity (depth and integration of processes) and superior financial performance.
* **Strategic Value:** ERM enhances a firm's ability to navigate uncertainties, capitalize on opportunities, and improve long-term resilience, serving as a source of competitive differentiation.

**Organizational Agility (OA) as a Value Driver**
* **Responsiveness:** In volatile, uncertain, complex, and ambiguous (VUCA) environments, the ability to respond rapidly to unpredictable customer needs is a crucial characteristic.
* **Dynamic Capabilities:** Agility is linked to the ability to sense opportunities/threats, seize opportunities, and transform operations.
* **Innovation Cycle:** Agile organizations drive faster product development and process improvements, which is essential for maintaining market relevance and customer satisfaction.

**Entrepreneurial Orientation (EO) and Startup Logic**
* **Core Components:** EO is characterized by risk-facing behavior, innovation-embracing behavior, and proactiveness (reacting before competitors).
* **Risk-Taking Logic:** 
    * High-profit potential is often linked to a higher risk tolerance, leading firms to choose more difficult projects.
    * Investing in high-risk projects prevents over-reliance on "tried-and-true" activities and integrates risk directly into the business model.
* **Survival vs. Growth Logic:**
    * Long-term competitiveness is derived from generating core resources through learning.
    * However, for startups facing immediate survival challenges, the "orientation logic of value activity formation through adaptive mechanisms" is more critical for short-term survival.
* **Performance Drivers:** Firms with high EO typically perform better financially due to their ability to take calculated risks, engage in R&D, and proactively seize market opportunities.

**Business Model Innovation (BMI) Strategies**
*   **Novelty-centered BMI:** Focuses on creating unprecedented value propositions, revenue models, and market offerings by introducing new elements or recombining existing ones. It targets market disruption and differentiation, typically requiring higher investment in R&D and creative processes.
*   **Efficiency-centered BMI:** Focuses on optimizing existing resources, processes, and resource utilization to achieve cost leadership and operational excellence.
    *   **Logic of Value Capture:** Aims to improve transactional efficiency and reduce transaction costs for counterparties.
    *   **Mechanisms for Performance:** Reduces complexity, uncertainty, and transaction risk. Key drivers include information sharing (to minimize information asymmetry) and real-time management of value systems (e.g., inventory monitoring, product traceability).
    *   **Example:** Uber's model reduces costs by avoiding ownership of expensive assets (cars, licenses), relying instead on platforms, contracts, and brand.

**Factors Influencing SME Performance (Investment Indicators)**
*   **Enterprise Risk Management (ERM):** Effective ERM allows SMEs to anticipate threats and opportunities, leading to better strategic decision-making and resource allocation. Well-defined risk response mechanisms reduce performance volatility and create a more stable business environment.
*   **Organizational Agility (OA):** The ability to quickly perceive opportunities and innovate products/services in response to customer needs. Agility in business processes is linked to superior financial performance and sustainable competitive advantage.
*   **Entrepreneurial Orientation (EO):** Dimensions such as innovativeness and risk-taking are positively related to firm performance. EO enables SMEs to proactively utilize resources for innovation, with more substantial effects observed in the long term.

**Business Model Innovation (BMI) Logic**

*   **Efficiency-Centered BMI:**
    *   **Logic:** Focuses on improving decision-making efficiency, reducing transaction costs, and streamlining processes to provide better products/services.
    *   **Value Proposition:** Achieves low-cost competitive advantages to compete against high-cost, low-efficiency rivals.
    *   **Impact:** Short-term cost reduction and improved transaction efficiency; long-term improvement in business performance.
    *   **Examples:** Southwest Airlines (point-to-point flights), Nucor (small steel mill efficiency), Spring Airlines.

*   **Novelty-Centered BMI:**
    *   **Logic:** Focuses on creativity and differentiation by introducing new elements or recombining existing ones to create unprecedented value propositions, revenue models, and market offerings.
    *   **Value Proposition:** Aims to disrupt existing markets or create entirely new market spaces to attract customers and capture added value.
    *   **Investment Considerations:**
        *   **Time Horizon:** May not be profitable in the short term; requires time to cultivate user habits and accumulate experience.
        *   **Evaluation:** Merits should not be judged by short-term financial indicators but by long-term accumulation and the ability to innovate over time.
        *   **Defensibility:** Once protection mechanisms are established, these models can be difficult for competitors to surpass in profitability.
    *   **Examples:** Dell (online customization), Disney (combining culture, technology, and multichannel cash), Apple (iPod–iTunes Store), JD.com (self-operated logistics model to enhance consumer experience).

**Drivers of Performance in SMEs/Startups**

*   **Dynamic Capabilities:** The ability to integrate, build, and reconfigure internal and external competences to address rapidly changing environments.
    *   **Enterprise Risk Management (ERM):** Foundation for identifying and mitigating risks; critical for stability and competitive advantage during adverse economic conditions.
    *   **Organizational Agility (OA):** High-level capability to respond efficiently to consumer needs and align corporate resources (assets, knowledge, relationships).
    *   **Entrepreneurial Orientation (EO):** Value orientation driving the pursuit of innovation, risk-taking, and proactive market behavior.
*   **The Mediating Role of BMI:** Dynamic capabilities (ERM, OA, EO) do not always directly improve performance; they often function through BMI as an intermediate vector. BMI transforms entrepreneurial initiatives and technological capabilities into structured, scalable business models that drive market success and sustained competitive advantage.

**Factors Influencing SME Performance and Business Model Innovation (BMI)**

*   **Types of Business Model Innovation (BMI):**
    *   **Efficiency-centered BMI:** Focuses on improving existing business models, optimizing production processes, reducing costs, and improving productivity of existing products.
    *   **Novelty-centered BMI:** Involves developing entirely new business models to explore new markets or products, creating new value propositions and market opportunities.

*   **Logic of Efficiency-centered BMI (Positive Impact on Performance):**
    *   **Resource Efficiency:** Streamlines operations, cuts costs, and allocates resources more effectively.
    *   **Sustainability:** Ensures that product and market strategy innovations remain profitable by focusing on cost reduction.
    *   **Risk Mitigation:** Balances the need for innovation with operational efficiency, which is particularly critical for resource-limited SMEs.
    *   **Short-term Gains:** Requires lower initial investment and produces faster results, allowing firms to accumulate capital and experience for future radical innovation.

*   **Logic of Novelty-centered BMI (Risks and Limitations):**
    *   **Market Misalignment:** May fail to address genuine market needs or customer preferences, especially in highly volatile environments.
    *   **Execution Risks:** Prone to failure due to ineffective project management, operational inefficiencies, or reliance on unproven technologies.
    *   **Brand Dilution:** Can create market confusion and dilute the brand.
    *   **Resource Constraints:** Implementing novelty-centered BMI is more challenging for SMEs due to financial, technological, and managerial experience constraints.
    *   **Risk Conflict:** There is a potential offset between Enterprise Risk Management (ERM), which avoids risk, and novelty-centered BMI, which embraces adventurous innovation.

*   **Dynamic Capabilities Driving Performance:**
    *   SME performance is enhanced through the combination of Enterprise Risk Management (ERM), Organizational Agility (OA), and Entrepreneurial Orientation (EO), specifically when mediated by efficiency-centered BMI.

**Competitive Advantage and Business Model Innovation (BMI)**
* **Source of Advantage:** Long-term competitive advantage is derived from rare, non-replicable technology or resources.
* **Role of BMI:** While BMI alone may not always have a positive effect on performance, it is necessary to design appropriate BMIs to promote technological and product innovation.
* **Barriers to Entry:** Pioneering a new business model can create a competitive advantage; although competitors may eventually replicate the model, the process can take years.
* **Strategic Positioning:** A combination of differentiation and cost leadership (e.g., through modular production and sales integration) can create a market advantage that is difficult for competitors to replicate.

**Risk Management and Operational Logic for SMEs**
* **Enterprise Risk Management (ERM):** Essential for identifying, assessing, and managing internal/external risks (policy, economic, environmental, social responsibility, and supply chain) while capitalizing on opportunities.
* **Supply Chain Resilience:** Logic for stability includes establishing a supplier management system, improving visibility via information technology, and maintaining alternate suppliers to ensure quick switching during primary supplier failure.
* **Market Integration:** Utilizing O2O (Online-to-Offline) models to create industrial ecosystems that combine physical logistics and customer experiences with digital processes and financial transactions.

**External Influences on Startup/SME Growth**
* **Policy Incentives:** Government support for high-tech and innovative SMEs (loans, training, tax relief) serves as a catalyst for traditional firms to transition into technology-based SMEs.
* **Technological Integration:** The shift toward technology-based models involves integrating big data, artificial intelligence, and e-commerce to improve configuration and service frameworks.
