**Research Note: Conservation Planning and Time Horizons**

*   **Source:** Hartig, F., & Drechsler, M. (2008). The time horizon and its role in multiple species conservation planning. *Biological Conservation*, 141, 2625-2631.
*   **Key Concept:** Survival probability within a specific time horizon (T) is a standard measure for population viability.
*   **Findings on Biodiversity Prioritization:**
    *   The choice of time horizon (T) acts as a form of "time preference" (similar to economic discounting), where conservation success is measured at T and subsequent effects are ignored.
    *   While the time horizon may not change the ranking of conservation options for a single species under stationary conditions, it can "substantially change conservation decisions" when planning for multiple species.
*   **Conclusion for Conservation Strategy:** It is critical to analyze the sensitivity of model results to the chosen time horizon or time preference measures when prioritizing biodiversity conservation efforts.
