Title: [2608.05179] Autonomous Research Agents: A Survey of AI Scientists and the Verification Gap

Content:

**Technical Bottlenecks and Failure Modes**
* **The Verification Gap:** A central bottleneck in autonomous research agents is the disparity between the ability to generate research outputs (e.g., paper-like manuscripts and runnable code) and the ability to verify the claims made within those outputs.
* **Lack of External Validation:** In a survey of 24 runnable systems, only 38% reported any novelty-verification method. Among "closed-loop L4" (high autonomy) systems, most are merely mechanical reruns, and none demonstrated an externally validated in-loop oracle.
* **Auditability Deficits:** While code release is common (83% of runnable systems), there is a significant lack of reproducibility-grade artifacts, such as seeds or execution traces (only 38% released).

**AI Agent Architectural Patterns & Lifecycle**
* **Research Lifecycle Integration:** Agents are being deployed across the full scientific lifecycle: ideation, literature search, experiment design/execution, analysis, manuscript drafting, and review.
* **Autonomy Levels:** The research identifies a spectrum of autonomy, specifically highlighting "closed-loop L4 systems" which aim for high levels of independence in the research process.

**Mitigation and Evaluation Strategies**
* **Audit Dimensions:** The research proposes seven dimensions for auditing AI agents:
    1. Lifecycle stage
    2. Autonomy level
    3. Evaluation method
    4. Released artifacts
    5. Human-in-the-loop points
    6. Novelty verification
    7. Result-selection disclosure
