**Gestation Length**
*   **Average:** 283 days (Range: 279–287 days).
*   **Variations:** Length varies by breed, calf sex, and litter size.

**Fetal Development Timeline**
*   **Early Embryo (Days 1–30):**
    *   Day 4–5: Embryo enters the uterus.
    *   Day 16–17: Maternal recognition of pregnancy occurs via secretion of interferon-tau to prevent luteolysis.
    *   Day 25–30: Placentation begins.
*   **First Trimester (Days 1–95):**
    *   Focus: Embryogenesis and organogenesis.
    *   Day 45: Transition from embryo to fetus.
    *   All major organ systems form; fetal programming occurs.
    *   Weight gain: 5–10% of birth weight.
*   **Second Trimester (Days 96–190):**
    *   Focus: Rapid fetal growth and skeletal/muscle fiber development.
    *   Muscle fiber numbers are permanently fixed during this stage.
    *   Weight gain: 20–25% of birth weight.
*   **Third Trimester (Days 191–283):**
    *   Focus: Rapid fetal weight gain, brain, and immune system development.
    *   Weight gain: Approximately 70% of total birth weight is gained in the final 90 days.
    *   Day 250: Colostrum begins forming in the udder.

**Nutritional Requirements per Trimester**
*   **First Trimester:** Requirements are only slightly elevated above maintenance.
*   **Second Trimester:** Cow can typically maintain Body Condition Score (BCS) with normal nutrition.
*   **Third Trimester:** Nutritional requirements increase significantly; energy and protein supplementation are critical.

**Labor and Calving (Parturition)**
*   **Process:** A three-stage labor process.
    *   Stage 1: Cervical dilation (2–6 hours).
    *   Stage 2: Active fetal expulsion (30 minutes–2 hours).
    *   Stage 3: Placental passage (within 12 hours).
*   **Post-Calving:** Calves require colostrum within 2–6 hours of birth for passive immunity transfer.

**Breed Comparisons (Puberty/Maturity)**
*While the text does not provide a gestation length comparison by breed, it notes maturity differences:*
*   **British Breeds (Angus, Hereford):** Earliest maturing (8–11 months).
*   **Dairy Breeds (Holstein, Jersey):** 9–12 months.
*   **Continental Breeds (Simmental, Charolais):** 10–13 months.
*   **Zebu/Bos indicus (Brahman):** Latest maturing (13–18 months).

**Gestation Length by Breed**
*   Brahman / Zebu: 292 days
*   Charolais: 289 days
*   Simmental: 287 days
*   Hereford: 285 days
*   Angus: 283 days
*   Jersey (Dairy): 279 days
*   *Note:* Individual variation of ±7 days is normal. Bull calves average 1–2 days longer gestation than heifer calves.

**Fetal Development & Nutritional Requirements**
*   **Second Trimester (Days 96–190):**
    *   Critical period for "fetal programming."
    *   Nutritional status (specifically protein and energy levels) directly influences the number of muscle fibers formed.
    *   Muscle fiber number is fixed permanently during this stage; nutritional stress leads to reduced lifetime muscle development, lower weaning weights, and inferior feedlot performance.
*   **Third Trimester (Days 191–283):**
    *   Rapid fetal weight gain: ~70% of birth weight is gained in the final 93 days.
    *   Development: Immune and nervous systems mature.
    *   Colostrum formation begins around day 250.
    *   Nutritional needs: Cow energy and protein requirements increase 25–35% above mid-gestation levels.

**Signs of Labor and Calving (Parturition)**
*   **Stage 1: Preparation (2–6 hours; up to 8 for heifers)**
    *   Signs: Cervical dilation, onset of uterine contractions, restlessness, isolation from the herd, appearance of the water sac.
*   **Stage 2: Fetal Expulsion (30 min–2 hours)**
    *   Signs: Active straining, visible amniotic sac, presentation of calf feet and head, followed by delivery.
*   **Stage 3: Placenta Expulsion (Within 12 hours of calving)**
    *   Events: Natural expulsion of the placenta and beginning of uterine involution.

Indicators of pregnancy:
- Non-veterinary indicators: Absence of return to estrus 18–24 days post-breeding; lack of heat when a cleanup bull is present; visible fetal movement or flank distension from approximately day 180+ of gestation.
- Diagnostic tests: Blood tests (BioPRYN PAG test) can detect pregnancy from day 28 onward.
