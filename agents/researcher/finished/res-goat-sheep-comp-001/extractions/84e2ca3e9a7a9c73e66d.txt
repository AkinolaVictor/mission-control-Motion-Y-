Title: DIETARY DIFFERENCES BETWEEN GOATS AND SHEEP
							| Proceedings of the New Zealand Grassland Association

Content:

Dietary Habits and Intake:
- Feed Intake: Goats in pens consume approximately 20% more low-quality feed than sheep.
- Energy Requirements: The higher intake in goats is offset by higher maintenance energy requirements, meaning they are not inherently better suited for low-quality feeds than sheep.
- Digestion: There is little difference between goats and sheep regarding the digestion and utilization of feeds.
- Foraging Behavior: 
    - Goats consume more browse (e.g., gorse) than sheep.
    - Under intensive pasture grazing, goats' dry matter intake declines more rapidly than sheep's as herbage mass decreases.
    - Goats show a greater unwillingness to graze less preferred components of the sward.

Agricultural Implications:
- Sheep are more effective than goats for grazing to low herbage masses; using goats in these conditions results in lower intake and performance.
