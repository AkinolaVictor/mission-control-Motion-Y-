**Agricultural and Farming Purposes (Goats)**
*   **Traditional Production:** Used globally for production, with a high concentration of populations in developing countries.
*   **Diversified Roles:** Use of goats is evolving beyond production to include:
    *   Educational purposes (e.g., keeping goats in elementary schools).
    *   Therapeutic and recreational uses.
    *   Companion animals/pets.
    *   Vegetation management (specifically in the USA).
*   **Production Systems:** 
    *   Extensive and organic systems are increasing in developed countries due to consumer demand for higher welfare.
    *   Challenges in developing countries often include difficult farming conditions affecting animal welfare.
*   **Non-Zootenic Management:** Increasing presence of goats in zoos, pet farms, sanctuaries, and private settings.

**Behavioral Traits (Goats)**
*   **Socialization:** Long-term socialization with humans significantly affects human-directed behavior.
*   **Learning and Training:** Goats are capable of shape discrimination learning and can be trained using conditioned reinforcement (e.g., clicker training for halter behavior).
*   **Environmental Interaction:** Behavioral responses are influenced by environmental enrichment, particularly in dairy goats in semi-arid regions.

**General Biological/Health Notes (Small Ruminants)**
*   **Disease Susceptibility:** Capripoxviruses represent an emerging worldwide threat affecting sheep, goats, and cattle.
