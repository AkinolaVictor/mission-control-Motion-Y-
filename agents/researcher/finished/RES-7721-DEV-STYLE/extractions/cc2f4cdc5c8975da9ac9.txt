Title: "Japanese Mothers' Parenting Styles with Preschool-Age Children" by Ai Shibazaki Lau

Content:

Study: "Japanese Mothers' Parenting Styles with Preschool-Age Children" (Ai Shibazaki Lau, 2006)

- Objective: To determine if Western parenting typologies (authoritative, authoritarian, permissive, and psychological control) and their dimensions (connection, regulation, physical punishment, verbal hostility) are measurable within the context of Japanese parenting.
- Participants: 214 Japanese mothers of preschool-age children (101 boys, 113 girls) in Kushiro-city, Japan.
- Findings on Typologies:
    - Authoritative and Authoritarian: Confirmed as measurable via a 23-item, five-factor model.
    - Psychological Control: Confirmed as measurable via a 9-item, two-factor model, specifically identifying "shaming" and "directiveness" as measurable constructs in Japan.
    - Permissive Parenting: An invariant measurement model could not be identified for this style.
- Key Observations:
    - Shaming and directiveness were associated with dimensions of both authoritative and authoritarian parenting.
    - No significant gender differences were found in parenting patterns between boys and girls.
