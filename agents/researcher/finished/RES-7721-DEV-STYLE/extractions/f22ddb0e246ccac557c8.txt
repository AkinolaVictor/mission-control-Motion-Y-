Title: Relationship Between Parenting Styles and Psychological Well-Being Among Adolescents : An Analysis of Authoritative, Authoritarian, Permissive, and Uninvolved Parenting
							| PROFESSIONAL HEALTH  JOURNAL

Content:

**Study Overview**
*   **Objective:** Examined the relationship between parenting styles and psychological well-being among adolescents (n=192, aged 14–16) in a semi-rural Indonesian setting.
*   **Methodology:** Cross-sectional correlational design using the Parenting Style Four Factor Questionnaire (PS-FFQ) and the Ryff Psychological Well-Being Scale.

**Key Findings**
*   **Correlation:** A highly significant relationship exists between parenting style and psychological well-being ($\chi^2 = 180.999$; $df = 6$; $p < 0.001$).
*   **Authoritative Parenting:** 
    *   Most prevalent style in the sample (65.1%).
    *   Consistently associated with higher levels of psychological well-being.
    *   Identified as a "strong protective factor" for adolescent emotional health.
*   **Other Parenting Styles:** Authoritarian (16.7%), permissive (10.9%), and uninvolved (7.3%) styles were associated with lower levels of psychological well-being.

**Conclusions**
*   Supportive, structured, and communicative parenting practices (authoritative) are critical for promoting adolescent emotional health.
