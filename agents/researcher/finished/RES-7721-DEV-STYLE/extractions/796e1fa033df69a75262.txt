Title: Nurturing the Self: Investigating Self-Compassion as a Moderator Between Percieved Paretning Styles and Mental Health Outcomes in Adulthood

Content:

Study: Nurturing the Self: Investigating Self-Compassion as a Moderator Between Perceived Parenting Styles and Mental Health Outcomes in Adulthood (Storm, M. J., Cape Breton University).

- Participants: 316 adults (ages 18–67, Mean = 27.75).
- Measured Variables: Perceived parenting styles (PAQ), self-compassion (SCS), anxiety (GAD-7), and depression (BDI-II).

Findings on Parenting Styles and Adult Mental Health:
- Authoritarian Parenting: Predicted higher levels of depression (b = 0.22, p < .001) and anxiety (b = 0.10, p = .006).
- Permissive Parenting: Showed no significant effects on the measured mental health outcomes.

Moderating Factors:
- Self-Compassion: Predicted lower depression (b = –9.17, p < .001) and lower anxiety (b = –4.57, p < .001).
- Interaction: A marginal interaction (b = –0.17, p = .053) suggests self-compassion may buffer depressive symptoms specifically for individuals from authoritarian backgrounds.
