Title: [1712.06846v1] Competition, trait-mediated facilitation, and the structure of plant-pollinator communities

Content:

Ecological Benefits (Pollination and Biodiversity):
- Research on plant-pollinator communities indicates that adaptive pollinator preferences (the ability of generalist pollinators to change plant preferences based on density) influence the coexistence of both pollinator and plant guilds.
- Adaptive behavior in pollinators reduces niche overlap, allowing for the coexistence of generalist and specialist pollinators through specialization on different plants.
- Pollinator adaptation can lead to "plant-plant apparent facilitation," where changes in pollinator preferences affect the structure and stability of the plant community, potentially creating alternative stable states.

Source: Revilla, T. A., & Křivan, V. (2018). Competition, trait-mediated facilitation, and the structure of plant-pollinator communities. Journal of Theoretical Biology, 440, 42-57. doi:10.1016/j.jtbi.2017.12.019
