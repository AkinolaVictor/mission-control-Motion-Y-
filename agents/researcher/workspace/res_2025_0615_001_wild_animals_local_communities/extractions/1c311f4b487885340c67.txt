**Research Notes: Ecological-economic modelling of interactions between wild and commercial bees and pesticide use**

*   **Ecological Benefits & Ecosystem Services:**
    *   Pollination and pest control are identified as essential inputs for farm output.
    *   Wild pollinators provide critical ecosystem services that support agricultural productivity.

*   **Threats to Wild Pollinators:**
    *   **Pesticide Use:** Increasing pesticide use associated with changing farm practices is linked to the decline of wild pollinator populations.
    *   **Commercial Competition:** The introduction of commercial bees as alternatives to wild pollinators can lead to further decline and potential local extinction of wild bee populations.
    *   **Economic Drivers:** The drive to increase farm output can inadvertently cause local declines in wild bee populations.

*   **System Dynamics & Sustainability:**
    *   **Threshold Behavior:** Transitions between population stability and local extinction are characterized by threshold behavior, making them difficult to predict or detect in advance.
    *   **Sensitivity:** Small changes in economic parameters (e.g., input prices) and ecological parameters (e.g., wild bee carrying capacity and pesticide effects) can push the system beyond the extinction threshold.
    *   **Recovery Potential:** Re-establishment of wild bees following local extinction may be possible by increasing the price of pesticides or decreasing the price of commercial bees.
    *   **Management Approach:** The study emphasizes the necessity of combining ecological modelling with economic analysis to inform the sustainable management of ecosystem service providers.
