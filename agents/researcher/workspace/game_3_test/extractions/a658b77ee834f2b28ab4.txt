- FY 1985 R&D planning document describes an “Enhanced Analysts Access to Large Data Bases” project for analysts in DDI, DDO, and NPIC, with information processing personnel in DDA.
- Analysts are expected to access large, growing volumes of diverse information stored under various automated database management systems across NDS, SAFE, VM, and other computing environments.
- Higher-order query capabilities allow analysts to work with complex information concepts; these concepts are automatically translated into a network.
- That network is used to generate potentially large or complex series of searches through appropriate databases by automatically generating queries to one or more database management systems.
- Returned data are combined using rules of formal logic and reasoning to answer the original high-level question.
- Graphical representation of facts overlaid on maps is used to aid understanding of large databases.
- The planned effort integrates previously developed automated logic processing capabilities (e.g., DADM) and workstation prototypes (e.g., Golden Tiger) with newly developed query and presentation techniques (e.g., natural language, symbolic or graphical input/output, computer-generated maps).
- New methods for controlling distributed data and merging data from central reference files with local analyst working files are to be investigated.
- The project includes an architectural study and design of modular approaches to integrating various user interfaces with a variety of underlying database management systems.
- Related ORD research programs in Intelligent Data Access, Symbolic Representation of Information, and the Golden Tiger Workstation during FY82/83 indicated potential for improving analyst access to large databases; FY83 geographic/cartographic database research addresses integration of geographic factual data with computer-generated maps, with distribution of cartographic information among mainframe and workstation investigated in FY83/84.
