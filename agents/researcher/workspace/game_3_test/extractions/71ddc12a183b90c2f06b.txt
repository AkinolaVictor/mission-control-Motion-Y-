Title: [2010.13501] Hierarchical Neural Architecture Search for Deep Stereo Matching

Content:

- Paper: "Hierarchical Neural Architecture Search for Deep Stereo Matching" (arXiv:2010.13501, cs.CV), submitted 26 Oct 2020; accepted at NeurIPS 2020.
- Authors: Xuelian Cheng, Yiran Zhong, Mehrtash Harandi, Yuchao Dai, Xiaojun Chang, Tom Drummond, Hongdong Li, Zongyuan Ge; Cheng and Zhong contributed equally.
- NAS is used to reduce human effort in neural network design; its underlying idea is to let the network choose among a set of operations (e.g., convolutions with different filter sizes) to find an architecture better adapted to the problem.
- NAS had succeeded in high-level vision tasks such as classification and semantic segmentation, but not yet in low-level geometric vision tasks such as stereo matching.
- State-of-the-art deep stereo matching networks are very large; directly applying NAS to them is computationally prohibitive with mainstream computing resources.
- Proposed first end-to-end hierarchical NAS framework for deep stereo matching, incorporating task-specific human knowledge into the NAS framework.
- Follows the standard deep stereo matching pipeline: feature extraction -> feature volume construction and dense matching; jointly optimizes architectures across the entire pipeline.
- Reported results: searched network outperforms all state-of-the-art deep stereo matching architectures; top-1 accuracy on KITTI stereo 2012, KITTI stereo 2015, and Middlebury benchmarks; top-1 on SceneFlow dataset; substantial improvements in network size and inference speed.
