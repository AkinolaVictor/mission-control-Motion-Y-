Content:

- State space search searches through a tree of possible states to find a goal state; planning algorithms search trees of goals and subgoals, attempting to find a path to a target goal (means-ends analysis).
- Simple exhaustive searches are rarely sufficient for real-world problems because the search space grows astronomically, making search too slow or non-terminating; heuristics/rules of thumb can prioritize choices more likely to reach a goal.
- Adversarial search is used for game-playing programs such as chess or Go; it searches through a tree of possible moves and countermoves for a winning position.
- Local search uses mathematical optimization to find a solution, beginning with a guess and refining it incrementally.
- Gradient descent is a local search that optimizes numerical parameters by incrementally adjusting them to minimize a loss function; variants are commonly used to train neural networks through backpropagation.
- Evolutionary computation iteratively improves candidate solutions by mutating and recombining them, selecting only the fittest to survive each generation.
- Distributed search processes can coordinate via swarm intelligence algorithms, including particle swarm optimization and ant colony optimization.
- Formal logic supports reasoning and knowledge representation; propositional logic operates on true/false statements with logical connectives, while predicate logic also operates on objects, predicates, relations, and quantifiers.
- Deductive reasoning can be structured as proof trees, with nodes labeled by sentences and children connected to parents by inference rules; problem-solving reduces to searching for a proof tree whose root is a solution and whose leaves are premises or axioms.
- With Horn clauses, problem-solving search can proceed forward from premises or backward from the problem; in general first-order clausal form, resolution solves by proving a contradiction from premises including the negation of the problem.
- Inference in Horn clause logic and first-order logic is undecidable and intractable; backward reasoning with Horn clauses underpins Prolog, is Turing complete, and is competitive in efficiency with other symbolic programming languages.
- Fuzzy logic assigns a degree of truth between 0 and 1 for vague or partially true propositions.
- Non-monotonic logics, including logic programming with negation as failure, handle default reasoning.
- Probabilistic methods for uncertain reasoning include decision theory, decision analysis, information value theory, Markov decision processes, dynamic decision networks, game theory, and mechanism design.
- Bayesian networks support reasoning, learning, planning, and perception; probabilistic algorithms can filter, predict, smooth, and find explanations for data streams (e.g., hidden Markov models or Kalman filters).
- Classifiers use pattern matching to determine the closest match; examples include decision trees, k-nearest neighbor, kernel methods such as support vector machines, naive Bayes, and neural networks.
- Neural networks have input, at least one hidden layer, and output; nodes apply functions and transmit data when weights cross thresholds; a network is typically called deep if it has at least two hidden layers.
- Neural network learning uses local search to choose weights; backpropagation is the most common training technique.
- Feedforward neural networks pass signals in one direction; perceptrons are typically single-layer; deep learning uses many layers; recurrent neural networks allow short-term memory; LSTMs better preserve long-term dependencies; convolutional neural networks use kernels for efficient local pattern processing.
- Deep learning uses multiple layers to progressively extract higher-level features from raw input.
- GPTs/LLMs generate text by predicting the next token; pretraining accumulates world knowledge; subsequent training often uses reinforcement learning from human feedback; current models can produce hallucinations.
- GPUs with AI-specific enhancements and specialized TensorFlow software replaced CPUs as the dominant means for large-scale machine learning model training in the late 2010s; Prolog was used in early AI research, while Python has become predominant; transistor density trends include Moore's law and faster GPU improvements sometimes called Huang's law.
