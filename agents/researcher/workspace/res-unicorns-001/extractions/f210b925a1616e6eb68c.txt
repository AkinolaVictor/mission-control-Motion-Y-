**Definition and Terminology**
*   **Unicorn:** A privately owned startup company valued at over US$1 billion. The term was coined in 2013 by venture capitalist Aileen Lee to represent the statistical rarity of such ventures.
*   **Decacorn:** A private company valued at over $10 billion.
*   **Centicorn/Hectocorn:** A private company valued at over $100 billion.
*   **Terracorn:** A private company valued at $1 trillion.

**Valuation Trends and Methods**
*   **Basis of Valuation:** Unlike established companies (valued on past performance), startup valuations are derived from growth opportunities and expected long-term development in a potential market.
*   **Valuation Drivers:** Usually result from large venture capital funding rounds or buyouts by larger companies (e.g., Facebook's $1 billion acquisition of Instagram).
*   **Common Methods:**
    *   Discounted cash flow analysis.
    *   Market comparable method.
    *   Comparable transactions.
*   **Market Sizing Process:** Involves defining market sub-segments, top-down sizing, bottom-up analysis, and competitor analysis to create financial forecasts.
*   **Trends:**
    *   **2021 Surge:** Described as "meteoric," with $71 billion invested in 340 new unicorns, exceeding the total of the previous five years.
    *   **2022 Decline:** Many valuations fell due to the COVID-19 pandemic economic slowdown, increased interest rates (raising borrowing costs), market volatility, and stricter regulatory scrutiny.
    *   **Current Status:** As of May 2024, CB Insights identified 1,248 unicorns worldwide.

**Growth Drivers**
*   **Blitzscaling (Get-Big-Fast):** A strategy of rapid expansion via large funding rounds and price cutting to capture market share and eliminate competitors.
*   **Corporate Buyouts:** Large public companies (e.g., Apple, Meta, Google) often acquire established technology/business models rather than developing them internally, especially in low-interest-rate environments.
*   **Increased Private Capital:**
    *   The average age of a tech company before going public increased from 4 years (1999) to 11 years.
    *   **JOBS Act (2012):** Increased the allowed number of shareholders by four times before public financial disclosure is required.
*   **Avoidance of IPOs:** Companies use repeated funding rounds to avoid the risks of public market devaluation (e.g., Square and Trivago were priced below initial offer prices) and stringent regulations like the Sarbanes–Oxley Act.
*   **Technological Advances:** Social media and new technologies allow startups to achieve economies of scale and expand faster.
*   **Sector Trends:** The "sharing economy" (e.g., Uber, Airbnb) and the shift from physical retail to e-commerce (e.g., Amazon, Alibaba) have been primary drivers.

**Risks and Limitations**
*   **Overvaluation:** Research by Stanford professors (2018) suggests unicorns are overvalued by an average of 48%.
*   **Sustainability:** Experts (e.g., Bill Gurley) have warned that excessive available capital creates a speculative and unsustainable environment, leading to "dead unicorns."
*   **Market Correction:** Frequent occurrence and stock price devaluation can lead to a loss of "luster" and investor confidence.

**Examples**
*   **Successful/Large Unicorns:** ByteDance, SpaceX, Stripe.
*   **Acquisition-based Unicorns:** Instagram, Dollar Shave Club (both acquired for $1 billion).
*   **Failed/Devalued IPOs:** Square, Trivago (market value dropped below private valuation upon IPO).

**Definitions and Valuation Tiers**
*   **Unicorn:** A privately held startup company valued at over $1 billion.
*   **Minicorn:** A startup with a valuation exceeding $1 million, typically characterized by having achieved product-market fit and substantial revenue growth.
*   **Soonicorn:** A fast-growing startup with the potential to reach a $1 billion valuation in the near future.
*   **Decacorn:** A startup valued above $10 billion.
*   **Hectocorn:** A startup valued above $100 billion.
*   **Kilocorn:** An informal term for a private startup valued at approximately $1 trillion.
*   **Camel Startup:** A term for startups capable of surviving unfavorable conditions with minimal resource expenditure.

**Growth Drivers and Operating Models**
*   **Network Orchestrators:** A business model common among unicorns and sharing economy startups. It relies on a network of peers creating value through interaction, sharing, collaboration, and peer-to-peer or business-to-person selling platforms.

**Valuation Trends and Risks**
*   **Overvaluation:** Research by Gornall and Strebulaev (Stanford University) found that reported post-money valuations of 135 U.S. unicorns averaged 48% above fair value, with some exceeding fair value by more than 100%.
*   **Market Risks:**
    *   **Capital Excess:** Excessive available capital can inflate valuations regardless of company merit, potentially reducing entrepreneur accountability to investors.
    *   **Burn Rates:** High burn rates and a "complete absence of fear" in investment climates are cited as precursors to "dead unicorns" (failed unicorns).
    *   **Policy Criticism:** Economic policies specifically targeting the creation of unicorns may overlook other societally beneficial forms of entrepreneurship.
