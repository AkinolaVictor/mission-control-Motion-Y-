Current Trends in Generative AI (GenAI) Application:
- Integration into innovation and marketing processes to revolutionize research and practice.
- Focus areas for current research:
    - Impact of GenAI on consumer behavior.
    - Alignment of consumer insights with firm-level marketing strategies.
    - Use of GenAI to analyze the relationship between market-based assets and firm value.
    - Analysis of consumer skills, preferences, and their evolving role in marketing processes.

**Generative AI (GenAI) Technical Overview & Trends**
*   **Definition:** GenAI consists of deep neural networks pre-trained on large datasets to create "foundation models," which are then fine-tuned to generate new content (text, images, video) based on human instructions.
*   **Technical Shift:** There is a transition from supervised learning (requiring labor-intensive human-annotated labels) to self-supervised learning.
    *   **Self-Supervised Learning:** Models are trained by predicting missing parts of data (e.g., masked words in a sentence or patches in an image), allowing them to scale to massive, unannotated datasets and develop a deeper understanding of context.
*   **Foundation Models & Architecture:**
    *   The **Transformer** architecture has enabled faster training and the emergence of foundation models.
    *   **Large Language Models (LLMs):** A subset of foundation models that function as conditional probability distributions over tokens. They generate text by iteratively sampling the next token based on a "prompt" (initial input tokens).
    *   **Characteristics:** Outputs are inherently random and conditional on the prompt, meaning the same prompt can produce multiple unique responses.
*   **Economic Barriers:** Training foundation models is highly resource-intensive (e.g., GPT-4 training costs are estimated at over $100 million), limiting development to a few companies. However, the cost of *using* these models is low, shifting firm competition from algorithm development to the ability to harness existing models.

**Industry Applications & Impact (2024 Context)**
*   **Marketing Productivity:** A McKinsey report projects GenAI could enhance marketing productivity by up to 15% of total expenditure, approximately $463 billion annually.
*   **Corporate Use Cases:**
    *   **Coca-Cola:** Used GenAI to co-create new beverages (e.g., Coca-Cola Sugar Y3000).
    *   **Unilever, Nestlé, and Mondelez:** Utilizing GenAI for advertising creation.
*   **Conceptual Classification:** GenAI is categorized as "feeling AI" (capable of emotional understanding and human interaction), distinguishing it from "mechanical AI" (repetitive tasks) and "thinking AI" (analytical decisions).

**Generative AI Capabilities and Trends**
*   **Creativity Mechanism:** GenAI's ability to generate novel and appropriate content stems from its training on extensive, diverse datasets, allowing it to connect weakly related concepts (novelty) while adhering to human-perceived standards of appropriateness (appropriateness).
*   **Performance vs. Humans:** A comparative study found that ideas generated by ChatGPT-4 exhibited higher average purchase intention scores than those generated by MBA students. The study noted that providing "good examples" in prompts did not significantly improve GPT-4's output, suggesting the model had already internalized "appropriate" ideas during training.
*   **Emergent Capabilities:** Key capabilities identified for innovation include:
    *   Idea generation
    *   Divergent thinking
    *   Analogical thinking
    *   Inductive reasoning
    *   Decision-making support (causal and logical reasoning)

**Current Limitations and State of Research**
*   **Hallucinations:** Models frequently produce factually incorrect or nonsensical content that appears plausible.
*   **Reasoning Gaps:** Current models show limited capabilities in making causal inferences.
*   **Lack of Embodiment:** Research attributes hallucinations and reasoning limitations to a lack of physical data, sensory stimuli, and grounded experience (most LLMs are trained exclusively on text).
*   **Multimodal Evolution:** The emergence of multimodal models, such as GPT-4V (Vision), which process text, image, and sound, is viewed as a potential path toward a more integrated understanding of the world.

**Industry Examples of Model Failures**
*   **Google Bard (Feb 2023):** Factually incorrect answer regarding the James Webb Space Telescope led to a significant drop in stock value.
*   **Meta Galactica:** A "large language model for science" withdrawn three days after release due to the production of scientific-sounding but factually wrong text.

Current trends in Generative AI (GenAI) application in innovation:
- GenAI is being utilized to provide consumers with diverse initial representations of solutions during the exploratory stage of innovation.
- Potential impact on creativity:
    - For conforming consumers: Access to a wider pool of GenAI-generated ideas may lead to more creative final solutions compared to no GenAI input.
    - For diversifying consumers: GenAI may act as a catalyst for further cognitive effort in the generative stage, encouraging the exploration of distant alternatives to increase final solution creativity.

**Current Trends in Generative AI (Marketing & Research)**

*   **Algorithmic Fidelity:** LLMs can be conditioned on simulated "individuals" with targeted identity and personality profiles to replicate attitudes and ideas across diverse demographic groups (e.g., gender, race, age). This allows for the generation of response distributions that correlate strongly with actual human survey data (Argyle et al., 2023).
*   **Substitution for Human Subjects/Experts:**
    *   **Consumer Preference Modeling:** GPT can generate responses exhibiting real-world customer behaviors, such as sloped demand curves and state dependency, providing a low-cost alternative to human-based conjoint studies (Brand et al., 2023).
    *   **Perceptual Mapping:** GPTNeo and GPT-4 have successfully matched perceptual maps derived from human surveys and replicated demographic and temporal differences (Li et al., 2024).
    *   **Expert Labeling:** ChatGPT-4 has demonstrated higher agreement with domain experts in classifying marketing mix variables in text (e.g., tweets) compared to human crowdsourced workers (Amazon mTurk) (Ringel, 2023).
*   **LLM Ensembling:** There is a trend toward assembling multiple LLMs to harness diverse strengths and mitigate idiosyncratic biases, as different models (trained on different datasets and architectures) show varying performance in tasks such as generating images for click-through rates (Hartmann et al., 2023).

**Limitations and Research Gaps**

*   **AI-Conformity Effect:** In co-ideation, consumers may conform to GenAI-proposed solutions, leading to "anchoring" and a decrease in collective creativity/innovation.
*   **Response Engineering:** Current GenAI is primarily designed to provide answers rather than ask probing questions to nudge users toward more creative deviations (Huang & Rust, 2023).
*   **Boundary Conditions:** The ability of LLMs to replace human subjects depends heavily on training data; they perform better for higher-involvement products or brands with more extensive review data in their training sets (Li et al., 2024).
*   **Algorithmic Bias:** LLMs may replicate gender, race, and economic biases present in their training data, though this "variety of biases" is what enables algorithmic fidelity.

**Industry Trends & Predictions**
* **Marketing Automation:** Gartner predicts that by 2025, Generative AI (GenAI) will produce 30% of brands' marketing messages.
* **Corporate Adoption:** Meta has announced intentions to use GenAI for the automatic creation of advertisements.
* **Regulatory Environment:** Governments (e.g., U.S. AI Disclosure Act) and social platforms (TikTok, YouTube) are increasingly enforcing the disclosure of AI-generated content.

**Technical Trends & Model Behavior**
* **Model Composition:** There is a growing argument that the full potential of GenAI is realized through the combination of different Large Language Models (LLMs) rather than any single individual model.
* **Control Parameters:** Research highlights three primary ways users control LLM behavior:
    * **Prompts:** Strategic input to guide output.
    * **Temperature:** Adjusts randomness/creativity. A temperature of 0 makes the model deterministic; higher temperatures increase diversity and creativity.
    * **Top_p Sampling (Nucleus Sampling):** Restricts the model's selection to a subset of tokens representing a specific probability mass (e.g., top 20%).
* **Observed Effects:** Preliminary analysis indicates that increasing temperature in ChatGPT-3 leads to more verbose output, which may correlate with higher evaluations in idea generation.

**State of GenAI Research (Marketing & Consumer Behavior)**
* **Persuasiveness:**
    * Findings are mixed: some studies show no difference between human and GenAI text, while others find ChatGPT-4 creates messages associated with higher satisfaction and willingness to pay than human experts.
    * GenAI-generated content is perceived by some as more factual and logical, but less likely to use vivid storytelling or express anger.
    * GPT-3 pro-vaccination messages were perceived as more positive, authentic, and colloquial.
* **Consumer Psychology:**
    * **Algorithm Aversion:** Humans often distrust algorithms even when they outperform humans, driven by perceived lower empathy, uniqueness neglect, and cost-cutting motives.
    * **Contextual Trust:** Aversion is lower for objective tasks (numerical estimation) and higher for tasks central to human identity (creativity).
    * **GenAI Specifics:** While GenAI is better at generating empathy than previous AI, its use in creative tasks may trigger negative responses because creativity is viewed as a quintessential human capability.
    * **Social Backlash:** Disclosure of GenAI use can trigger concerns beyond algorithm aversion, such as the fear that AI-generated diversity (e.g., in fashion modeling) deprives humans of opportunities.

**Industry Applications & Product Launches**
* **Coca-Cola "Real Magic" Platform:** A partnership with OpenAI utilizing GPT-4 and DALL·E, combined with Coca-Cola's historical archives, to allow users to generate original image cards. This initiative aims to lower the barrier for consumer participation in creative tasks by augmenting limited graphic design skills.

**Generative AI Trends & Research (2024)**
* **Human-AI Collaboration:** Current research is divided on whether GenAI substitutes or complements human expertise. Some evidence suggests a "human-in-the-loop" approach is necessary for sufficiency, while other findings indicate GPT-4 can generate more and better ideas than MBA students without human intervention.
* **Cost Efficiency:** LLMs have demonstrated the ability to achieve up to 91% cost savings in generating text-based SEO content.
* **Consumer Psychology:** There is an ongoing research focus on the "psychological ownership" of AI-generated content. While GenAI increases the reach of customer engagement initiatives by reducing cognitive barriers, it may decrease the sense of ownership and mental effort consumers feel, potentially impacting long-term engagement.
* **Corporate Strategy:** Firms pursuing cost-leadership strategies tend to use off-the-shelf GenAI to reduce marketing costs, whereas firms pursuing differentiation strategies are more likely to involve marketing departments in fine-tuning foundational models.

**Model Limitations (as of early 2024)**
* **Knowledge Cut-offs:** As of January 2024, some ChatGPT models had knowledge dates only reaching back to September 2023, limiting their ability to respond to rapid shifts in consumer preferences without up-to-date external market data.

**Current Trends and Research in Generative AI (GenAI)**

**Cognitive and Behavioral Impacts**
* **Cognitive Delegation:** There is emerging research concern that delegating cognitive tasks to GenAI may lead to permanent changes in brain structure and a decline in human cognitive abilities, similar to how GPS usage has impacted hippocampus functioning.
* **Creativity Erosion:** Researchers hypothesize that over-reliance on GenAI for content generation may diminish human involvement in creative cognitive processes and reduce the general appreciation for creativity.
* **Consumer Role Shift:** The role of the consumer is evolving from a "co-creator" to a "post-GenAI consumer," where the distinction between consumer and firm output blurs if both rely on the same AI inputs.

**Industry Application and Risks**
* **Fine-Tuning vs. Privacy:** Firms are exploring the technical feasibility of fine-tuning foundational models with proprietary data to better capture brand image and customer bases. However, this creates a tension between harnessing AI potential and risking the loss of competitive advantage through the exposure of proprietary knowledge.
* **Disinformation:** GenAI's ability to produce mass variations of content at negligible cost has increased the risk of coordinated disinformation campaigns targeting brand equity, sales, and stock performance.

**Technical Limitations and Model Behavior**
* **LLM Biases:** Large Language Models (LLMs) exhibit specific technical biases that affect output quality:
    * **Majority Label Bias:** Producing content that appears most frequently in the prompt.
    * **Recency Bias:** Favoring context located near the end of the prompt.
    * **Availability Bias:** Preferring answers frequent in the model's pre-training data.
* **Hallucinations:** Research is focused on understanding the "black-box" cognitive mechanisms of GenAI to predict and reduce instances of hallucination.
* **Prompt Engineering:** There is a call to adapt human-centric bias mitigation strategies (from survey research) to calibrate prompts and improve GenAI output.

**Tools Mentioned**
* **Midjourney and Stable Diffusion:** Identified as primary text-to-image tools for creating new objects from text prompts.

**Current Trends and Research in Generative AI (2023-2024)**

**LLM Capabilities and Applications**
*   **Idea Generation:** Research indicates the use of Large Language Models (LLMs) for idea generation in innovation processes (Girotra et al., 2023).
*   **Reasoning:** Recent studies explore inductive reasoning in LLMs compared to humans (Han et al., 2024) and the capacity of LLMs to act as zero-shot reasoners (Kojima et al., 2022).
*   **Perceptual Analysis:** Research is currently determining the validity of LLMs for automated perceptual analysis (Li et al., 2024).
*   **Economic Simulation:** LLMs are being utilized as simulated economic agents ("homo silicus") to derive economic insights (Horton, 2023).
*   **Scientific Research:** AI is being used to devise hypotheses to identify "blind spots" in traditional research (Hutson, 2023).

**Generative AI in Marketing and Creativity**
*   **Human Augmentation:** Generative AI is being applied to augment human creativity (Eapen et al., 2023).
*   **Visual Content:** Research is investigating whether generative AI can achieve human-level quality in visual marketing content (Hartmann et al., 2023).
*   **Persuasion:** LLMs are being tested for their ability to generate persuasive messaging, specifically for pro-vaccination campaigns (Karinshak et al., 2023).
*   **Creativity Benchmarks:** Some evidence suggests that top-performing humans still outperform AI in creative divergent thinking tasks (Koivisto & Grassini, 2023).

**Technical Model Trends**
*   **Ensembling:** Recent technical approaches include "LLM-blender," which uses pairwise ranking and generative fusion to ensemble multiple LLMs (Jiang et al., 2023), and reward-guided ensembles to route tasks to specific "expert" models (Lu et al., 2023).

**Risks and Limitations**
*   **Misinformation:** AI-generated text is identified as a significant tool for media misinformation (Kreps et al., 2022).
*   **Human Perception:** Research indicates that human heuristics for identifying AI-generated language are flawed (Jakesch et al., 2023).
